# 13: Device: look for chat peers on the tailnet

**What to build:** the device's Chat app finds the chat peers on the tailnet, as the Linux app does (story 1), instead of only taking a name or address typed in. The Contacts page has **Look for chat peers**; it probes each tailnet peer the device sees online on port 4810 with a hello, and lists those that answer, with their name and fingerprint and an **Add** each, which adds the peer as Add contact does with its address. Asked for by the user after the first bench run (ticket 12), where the laptop found the T-Deck but the T-Deck could not find the laptop.

**Blocked by:** none (ticket 03's contacts, ticket 04's dialling)

**Status:** done (hardware run to come)

- [x] The peers probed are the tailnet's online peers as the kernel already keeps them (`tailnet_manager.rs`, read by the Terminal's `tailscale peers` and Hermes's Address page), less this device. A probe is what the Linux app's is (`ChatPeer.probe`, `companion/core/lib/src/peer.dart`): connect, read the hello, close, nothing held as a request on the other side. Protocol, section 3 and "Further Notes" of the spec: the device probes only when its app asks, never in the background.
- [x] The probing runs off the UI and off the kernel worker, on a `kernel_task::spawn` task made for it and gone after (CLAUDE.md, "Internal RAM runs out first"); a few seconds' timeout a peer, a bounded number of peers, the device held awake meanwhile. The app shows that it is looking, and the results when they come (the kernel's generation counter, as the app's other pages).
- [x] Results leave out this device and peers that are already contacts (matched by key). Each shows its hello's name, its tailnet name and its fingerprint. Add adds it by the address it answered on, so its key is pinned at the first handshake as with Add contact.
- [x] Decisions (which peers to probe, which results to show) are host-tested in Rust; the probing in the simulator: `simulator/tests/test_tailchat.py` gains a case where the simulator's tailnet lists two reference peers on loopback (extend the simulator's pretend client, `THISTLE_SIM_TAILNET_PEERS`, as needed) and one non-chat address, the app looks, lists the two, and adds one.
- [x] LVGL's pool is 64 KB: the results are one `lv_table`, as the app's other lists.
- [x] CLAUDE.md's Tailnet chat lines say how discovery works on the device.

## Comments

Done on branch `tailnet-chat-13-discovery`.

- **Layout.** The decisions are a section of `tailnet_chat_policy.rs` ("Discovery"), host-tested in its own `discovery_tests`: `to_probe` (online, not this device's address, inside the tailnet's ranges or loopback in the simulator, each address once, at most `PROBE_MAX` = 24), `heard` (a version 1 hello's key and name; anything else is no chat peer), `to_show` (not this device's key, not a contact's key, not a pending contact added at that address or tailnet name on that port, each key once). The running of them is `tailnet_chat_discovery.rs`, a child module of `tailnet_chat.rs` (one `mod` line there), with its own state and C API, so it touches nothing of the listener, the dials or the State struct.
- **A look.** `tailnet_chat_discover()` reads `tailnet_manager::peers()`, picks with `to_probe`, and spawns `chat_look` (`kernel_task::spawn`, 8 KB internal stack), which holds the device awake (`chat discovery`) and probes one peer after another: a blocking connect (3 s), the upgrade, its hello once the 101 is in, their hello (3 s from the connect), a close 1000. Each answer bumps the generation; the log says `alice.tail-sim.ts.net at 127.0.0.2:4810 is a chat peer: alice-laptop <fp>`, `... no chat peer answered`, then `found 2 chat peers among 3 tailnet peers`. Results are kept until the next look and filtered when read, so a peer added drops out of the list at once.
- **C API** (a section at the end of `thistle/tailnet_chat.h`): `tailnet_chat_discover`, `tailnet_chat_get_discovery` (looking, looked, probed, peers), `tailnet_chat_found_count`, `tailnet_chat_get_found` (key, fingerprint, hello name, tailnet name's first label, address, port), `tailnet_chat_add_found` (by key: `add_contact` with the address it answered on and its hello's name).
- **The app.** Contacts has **Look for chat peers** last in its button row (so the earlier buttons' places did not move); it starts a look and opens a page `find`: "Looking: N of M tailnet peers asked", then one `lv_table` of the peers found (hello name; tailnet name, fingerprint), a tap on a row adding it and opening its contact page, and Look again. With none found it says that a tailnet policy keeping port 4810 closed looks the same as no chat peer.
- **Simulator.** `THISTLE_SIM_TAILNET_PEERS=NAME=IP[:offline],...` lists the pretend client's peers in place of the two made up (`peers_in_list`, host-tested). `test_tailchat.py --only app-discovery`: reference peers on 127.0.0.2 and 127.0.0.3 at a port set in system.json, a socket answering HTTP 404 on 127.0.0.4, and the simulator itself at 127.0.0.1; the app lists the two, neither saw a handshake nor holds a request, Add pins alice by key, and a second look lists bob alone.
- **This device** is left out of the probes by its address (the simulator's own entry is not probed), and of the results by key, which covers a device reachable at a second address (host-tested only).
- **Not seen on hardware**: probes through MicroLink (the first SYN to a peer with no WireGuard session is dropped, so a peer not talked to lately may take the 3 s connect to a second SYN, about 1 s), a peer whose policy drops port 4810 (3 s each), the internal RAM during a look.
